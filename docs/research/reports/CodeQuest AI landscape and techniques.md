# Ship the Mistake Memory Every Platform Lacks

CodeQuest AI's opening is not a smarter AI tutor but a memory. Across fifteen coding-practice platforms, **none detects which mistakes an individual learner keeps repeating and picks the next challenge to target them**, none explains a hidden-test failure without either leaking the test data or saying almost nothing, and none documents an enforced guard against its AI revealing solutions, while documented cases show AI help handing over full code or the fix. Those gaps map one-to-one onto problem statement E-02, and deterministic machinery closes them more reliably than a language model: tagged hidden tests, Python `ast` mistake detectors, an output-shape classifier, exception templates, a four-rung hint ladder with no "show solution" rung and hand-set knowledge tracing satisfy every clause, with the LLM demoted to rephrasing vetted text behind an executable leak check. The evidence explains the demotion: LLM help for novice programmers was correct in only 48–88% of cases across studies, general-purpose models told not to reveal answers still leaked them in 29–61% of tutoring dialogues, and a fine-tuned adversarial "student" extracted answers from teaching-tuned tutors 70–83% of the time. Learner code belongs in a fresh, network-less Docker container per test phase on a Linux VM, with a managed microVM (E2B or Modal) as fallback and Pyodide only for instant visible-test runs, because the free judge APIs student teams once relied on are closed or capped, and self-hosted Judge0 needs privileged containers on a legacy cgroup v1 host and suffered three critical sandbox escapes in 2024. Of the four extras, the skill map with a LeetCode roadmap and the unified portfolio are feasible and on-brief when they read from the same mistake model; the LeetCode extension is a fragile, terms-exposed stretch goal that should stay a thin, click-triggered panel; and the typing-speed trainer is off-brief and should shrink to a two-to-four-hour "syntax warm-up" or be cut. The US$2.50 OpenAI credit buys roughly **10,000–14,000 calls** on a nano-class model, so cost is not the constraint; reliability and leakage are, which calls for hard per-project spend caps, capped output tokens, schema-locked outputs, pre-generated cached rephrasings and a free-provider-then-template fallback chain.

## Fifteen platforms grade code, and none remembers your mistakes

Coding-practice platforms, AI tutors, portfolio aggregators and code-typing trainers each deliver one slice of what E-02 asks for. None joins the slices into a picture of the individual learner, and the places where each falls short line up closely with the problem statement's requirements.

### Hidden tests either stay silent or leak their data

When a submission fails, judges fall into three camps. Contest-style judges return a verdict and stop at the first failing test: Kattis runs the samples first and then says "we do not share any additional information regarding the content of the secret testcases" ([Kattis support](https://support.kattis.com/support/solutions/articles/79000030851-my-submissions-are-getting-rejected-what-can-i-do-)), CSES's course text says "only the first error will be shown" ([CSES](https://cses.fi/89/text/674)), and Codeforces reports "wrong answer on test N", with long inputs cut off behind an unclickable ellipsis even after the contest ([Codeforces blog](https://codeforces.com/blog/entry/104970); [Codeforces blog](https://mirror.codeforces.com/blog/captainkhan)). HackerRank lists every test, but a hidden one "appears with a lock icon" and shows "only execution results" ([HackerRank knowledge base](https://hackerrank-knowledge-base.help.usepylon.com/articles/3245197419)), and Codewars keeps its full suite "hidden until you solve the kata" ([Codewars docs](https://docs.codewars.com/getting-started/solving-kata)). At the other extreme, LeetCode sometimes shows the failing input for a Wrong Answer ([dev.to](https://dev.to/alex_hunter_44f4c9ed6671e/why-my-leetcode-solution-passes-some-test-cases-but-fails-others-1k7p)), and CodeChef has sold "WA Test Cases" as a paid Pro feature since August 2022 ([CodeChef Discuss](https://discuss.codechef.com/t/introducing-codechef-pro/102300)). Exercism sidesteps the dilemma by having no hidden tests: every test returns pass, fail or error next to its test code, inside a single 20-second, 3 GB window whose timeout halts the whole run ([Exercism test-runner spec](https://exercism.org/docs/building/tooling/test-runners/interface)).

Learner complaints show that the real problem is a lack of direction, not hidden tests as such. CodeChef users ask for failing cases because "it becomes very difficult to understand what is wrong with code", and a moderator replies that they "must deduce them yourself" ([CodeChef Discuss](https://discuss.codechef.com/t/include-a-process-to-view-the-failing-test-cases/20541); [CodeChef Discuss](https://discuss.codechef.com/t/will-codechef-provide-test-cases/125/40)). The same community warns that seeing the failing case means "you wont improve/learn much about test case generation and thinking about corner cases" ([CodeChef Discuss](https://discuss.codechef.com/t/practice-on-codechef/75340)), and Kattis's only advice after a secret-test failure is to write your own corner-case tests. The middle position that resolves this tension is unoccupied: naming the category of edge case a hidden test covers and how the code failed on it, without showing the input. **No platform reviewed documents separate wrong-output, runtime-error and timeout outcomes for each hidden test**; HackerRank's documented per-test status is simply passed or failed.

### AI help is metered, paywalled and measurably leaky

AI help is now standard on commercial platforms and almost always rationed. LeetCode gives Premium users "500 extra monthly credits" for its Ask Leet assistant and in release 2026.5 added a Quick Fix that analyzes and fixes code from the editor ([LeetCode release notes](https://leetcode.com/discuss/post/5736503/feature-release-notes-by-leetcode-9awg/)); CodeChef caps AI Help for free users ([CodeChef Discuss](https://discuss.codechef.com/t/ai-help-issue/118838)); NeetCode bundles hints and AI debugging into a Pro plan at $119 a year ([NeetCode Pro](https://neetcode.io/pro)); and CodeSignal's Cosmo launched with five "energy bars", one spent per question and one recharged every four hours unless the learner pays up to $24.99 a month ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/02/08/codesignal-launches-a-learning-platform-with-an-ai-powered-guide/)). **No platform publishes an enforced mechanism that stops its AI from revealing solutions**, and the documented cases point the other way. A CodeChef user reported in April 2025 that AI help "gives me the full code" ([CodeChef Discuss](https://discuss.codechef.com/t/ai-code-help-giving-entire-solution/123350)); NeetCode markets hints that "guide you toward the solution without giving it away" while its debugger shows "a side-by-side diff of your code and the suggested fix" ([NeetCode Pro](https://neetcode.io/pro)); and CheckiO's human-written hint ladder ends with a step that "shows the actual solution" ([CheckiO blog](https://py.checkio.org/blog/hints-for-mission/)). The anti-copy levers in use are incentives, such as Codewars forfeiting honor when solutions are unlocked ([Codewars docs](https://docs.codewars.com/concepts/kata/solutions/)) and GeeksforGeeks deducting marks for hints ([GfG rule book](https://www.geeksforgeeks.org/problem-of-the-day/rule-book)), plus AI bans that apply only during contests.

Academic tutors document real guards, and every one of them works outside the prompt. Harvard's CS50 Duck screens requests for "atypical nonalphanumeric patterns" before a second model call judges whether they are injection attempts, and it rations use with ten "hearts" that regenerate one every three minutes ([CS50 paper](https://cs.harvard.edu/malan/publications/V1fp0567-liu.pdf)). CodeHelp's authors found their models "strongly biased towards providing a complete solution ... even when the prompt requests otherwise", so they generate two completions, keep the one with less code, and run a third prompt to rewrite out any code that survives ([CodeHelp](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.06921)). CodeAid hides generated code behind pseudo-code, yet 53% of 77 sampled "Help Write Code" responses still gave the exact solution in pseudo-code form ([CodeAid](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.11314v2)). Consumer study modes are weaker still: ChatGPT Study Mode is built from "custom system instructions" rather than a new model ([InfoQ](https://www.infoq.com/news/2025/08/study-mode-chatgpt)), its top rule ("DO NOT DO THE USER'S WORK FOR THEM") was extracted on launch day, and learners can simply leave it switched off ([Simon Willison](https://simonwillison.net/2025/Jul/29/openai-introducing-study-mode)).

Measured performance shows why prompts alone fail. Codex explained novice Python errors correctly for only **48% of inputs** and produced correct fixes for 33% ([Leinonen et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2210.11630)); GPT-3.5 found every issue in a help request only 57% of the time, with false positives in about 40% ([Hellas et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2306.05715)); and CodeAid's debugging help was correct 63% of the time across a 700-student course ([CodeAid](https://arxiv.org/html/2401.11314v2)). Leakage is the default: in math tutoring dialogues GPT-4o gave away the solution in **35.2%** of conversations and Qwen2.5-72B in 61.0%, and only Google's pedagogy-tuned LearnLM stayed under 3%, at the price of almost no gain in student solve rates ([Dinucu-Jianu et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2505.15607v2)). Against adversarial students, persuasion beats hacking: contextual manipulation extracted answers 74% of the time against 50% for direct requests, and a fine-tuned attacker broke "pedagogically aligned" tutors **70–83%** of the time ([Zhao et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.18660v1)). Small models fail on every axis; in one benchmark GPT-4 wrote good, solution-concealing hints 89% of the time while a 4-bit Phi-3 running in the browser managed 8% ([Hints-In-Browser](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.05053v2)).

Learning outcomes give a second reason for restraint. Among about 1,000 Turkish high-school students, unrestricted ChatGPT-style help lifted practice scores by 48% but left students **17% worse** on the later unassisted exam, while a hint-giving tutor removed the harm without producing a gain ([Wharton Knowledge](https://knowledge.wharton.upenn.edu/article/without-guardrails-generative-ai-can-harm-education)). In Anthropic's 2026 randomized trial, developers learning a new Python library with AI scored 50% on a follow-up quiz against 67% for those coding by hand, with the widest gap on debugging questions, although participants who used AI only for conceptual questions scored 65% or more ([Anthropic research](https://www.anthropic.com/research/AI-assistance-coding-skills)). In a 257-student CS1 trial nearly all the benefit of GPT-4 compiler-error feedback vanished once the feedback was switched off ([Zhou et al.](https://learninganalytics.upenn.edu/ryanbaker/ICCE2025_paper_28.pdf)), although an earlier C# study found benefits persisting on five of six error types ([Pankiewicz & Baker](https://arxiv.org/abs/2403.12737)). The defensible reading is that guarded, conceptual help that fades over time protects learning, and answer-giving help undermines it.

### Portfolios count outputs, and typing trainers are a commodity

Cross-platform portfolios sell totals. Codolio, a Bangalore start-up founded in 2024, aggregates LeetCode, GeeksforGeeks, InterviewBit, CodeChef and Codeforces into cross-platform streaks, contest-rating charts and topic tallies ([Codolio LinkedIn](https://in.linkedin.com/company/codolio)), and its browser extension has about 3,000 users ([Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/hhldiohknhejgggdehdeepiggieflfjo)); README cards such as LeetCard render the same counts as images ([LeetCode-Stats-Card](https://github.com/JacobLinCool/LeetCode-Stats-Card)). The category's veteran, StopStalk, shut down on 1 August 2025, explaining that its data was "primarily built on crawling ... A platform can any day choose to block us" and that rankings were "skewed" whenever crawls missed users ([StopStalk](https://www.stopstalk.com/)). **None of these products reports process evidence**: attempts before acceptance, which mistake classes recurred and were fixed, or how many hints a learner needed.

Code-typing trainers are a mature niche with settled conventions. typing.io drills open-source files and counts symbols and backspace, "resulting in uninflated WPMs" ([typing.io](https://typing.io/)); SpeedCoder imports a learner's own code from GitHub and reports the keys missed most ([SpeedCoder](https://www.speedcoder.net/)); and the MIT-licensed SpeedTyper.dev races snippets from real open-source projects ([SpeedTyper.dev](https://github.com/codicocodes/speedtyper.dev)). The big typing sites stay prose-first, and Monkeytype's `code_python` mode is a list of 174 single tokens such as `append` and `__init__`, not code ([Monkeytype](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/monkeytypegame/monkeytype/master/frontend/static/languages/code_python.json)). None links its drills to a learner's programming errors.

The table condenses where each E-02 requirement stands today.

| E-02 requirement | Closest existing practice | Where it falls short |
|---|---|---|
| Restricted execution | Exercism documents no network, 20 s and 3 GB; CSES runs the Isolate sandbox | Most platforms publish no limits |
| A separate outcome for every test | HackerRank lists every test; Exercism reports pass, fail or error per test | Kattis, CSES and Codeforces stop at the first failure; nobody separates wrong output, runtime error and timeout on hidden tests |
| Hidden tests never revealed | Kattis, CodingBat, HackerRank's lock icon | LeetCode sometimes shows the failing input; CodeChef sells it |
| Plain-language failure explanation | Exercism's deterministic analyzers | Every AI explainer found is paywalled or metered |
| Progressive hints that never reveal the solution | CheckiO's authored ladders; GfG's hint costs | CheckiO ends in the solution; NeetCode's debugger shows the fix |
| Recurring mistakes per learner, adapting future challenges | None | Exercism analyzes one submission at a time; Codewars adapts to rank, CodeSignal to stated goals |
| Concept-level skill tracking | Exercism's concept tree; CodeSignal's skills profile | Both track completion, not estimated mastery |

Exercism comes closest with deterministic tooling. Its analyzers attach typed comments (an "essential" comment soft-blocks the student) and concept tags such as `technique:recursion`, and its representers normalise solutions so that one mentor comment reaches every structurally identical submission, a system that had delivered instant feedback on more than 800,000 solutions by April 2023 ([Exercism analyzers](https://exercism.org/docs/building/tooling/analyzers/interface); [Exercism representers](https://exercism.org/docs/building/tooling/representers); [Exercism blog](https://exercism.org/blog/insiders-preview)). Both still work one submission at a time. Codewars' training routines adapt to rank ([Codewars docs](https://docs.codewars.com/concepts/kata/training-routines)), CodeSignal's Cosmo builds a path from stated goals and level ([TechCrunch](https://techcrunch.com/2024/02/08/codesignal-launches-a-learning-platform-with-an-ai-powered-guide/)), and of the platforms reviewed only Exercism and CSES, which runs the Isolate sandbox, were found documenting their execution limits ([CSES paper](https://ioinformatics.org/journal/v14_2020_105_111.pdf)). The contrast for judges fits in one line: competitors adapt to what a learner has completed, and CodeQuest adapts to what a learner keeps getting wrong.

## Deterministic code can satisfy every clause of E-02

The rule that follows from this evidence is simple: **every decision E-02 makes testable belongs to deterministic code**, and the LLM may touch only wording. Verdicts, hidden-test secrecy, diagnosis, hint gating, mistake detection, skill estimates and the final decision on whether a hint may be shown all have cheap, reproducible implementations in the Python standard library plus a few permissively licensed packages. The systems that worked in production wrap the model in static analysis or rule-based filters rather than trusting it; JetBrains' learning-plugin hints "combine static code analysis from your IDE with the power of large language models", and the research behind them uses static analysis to cap the size of any code in a hint ([JetBrains blog](https://blog.jetbrains.com/education/2025/06/02/ai-hints-plugin/); [Birillo et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2410.09268)). Built this way, three capabilities would be unique in the market: hidden-test failures explained by category and outcome class, a mistake memory that changes the next challenge, and a hint ladder whose no-solution ceiling is enforced by running code.

### Tagged hidden tests explain failures without exposing inputs

The most valuable design decision costs almost nothing: tag every test, visible and hidden, from a fixed vocabulary such as `empty`, `single`, `boundary`, `negative`, `duplicates`, `large` and `base`. A failing hidden test then reports only its index, outcome class and category, so the explanation can say "hidden test 4 (edge case: empty input) raised IndexError on line 7" without revealing a value. Exercism's results format is a ready template ([Exercism test-runner spec](https://exercism.org/docs/building/tooling/test-runners/interface)): keep its per-test name, status, message and 500-character output cap and its rules for cleaning tracebacks (shorten paths, collapse frames outside the learner's code, never show a stack without its message); widen status to passed, wrong output, runtime error and timeout; add visibility and category fields; and emit test code and output only for visible tests. Give each test its own timeout of about two seconds so one infinite loop cannot wipe out the other results, the weakness of a single whole-run window.

Plain-language explanations should come from templates rather than generation. The evidence for enhanced error messages is mixed: Becker's controlled study of about 200 Java students found fewer errors per compiler message (median 18 against 34) and fewer repeated errors (9,752 against 13,849) ([Becker 2016](https://researchrepository.ucd.ie/rest/bitstreams/22170/retrieve)), while Denny and colleagues found no effect among 83 students ([Denny et al.](https://api.crossref.org/works/10.1145%2F2591708.2591748)). Error frequencies are steeply skewed, so a dozen templates cover most cases; of 643,248 Python errors on CS Circles, `SyntaxError: invalid syntax` accounted for **27.9%**, `NameError` for 15.1% and `EOFError` for 11.8% ([Pritchard 2015](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1509.07238)). The EOFError row matters because in a function-style exercise it signals a learner calling `input()`, which a static check catches before anything runs. Each template should follow the ITiCSE working group's guidance on context, low cognitive load and positive tone ([It Will Never Work in Theory](https://neverworkintheory.org/2021/09/02/compiler-error-messages-considered-unhelpful.html)): state what happened, show where (the line and sub-expression, located with `ast`), cite runtime evidence ("`i` was 5, but `nums` has 5 items, positions 0–4"), and end with a question rather than a fix. On hidden tests the template shows only the exception type and the learner's line, never argument values. Python 3.12 already adds hints such as "Did you forget to import 'x'?" ([What's New in Python 3.12](https://docs.python.org/3/whatsnew/3.12.html)); the MIT-licensed friendly-traceback can cover untemplated exceptions once its 3.12 support is checked ([friendly-traceback](https://github.com/friendly-traceback/friendly-traceback)), while PythonTA's GPL-3.0 license makes it a reference rather than a dependency ([PythonTA](https://github.com/pyta-uoft/pyta)).

Wrong answers get the same treatment. A rule-ordered classifier comparing return values can label most wrong outputs in microseconds: `None` returned while the expected value appears in captured stdout means the learner printed instead of returning; an equal value of the wrong numeric type points at `/` versus `//`; output equal to the expected list minus its last element means a dropped final element; a difference of exactly one suggests off-by-one; and identical output across three different inputs suggests a hard-coded answer. No published evaluation of such classifiers was found, so a label should feed the learner model only when it recurs on at least two failing tests.

### A mistake counts as recurring only when static and dynamic evidence agree

Published misconception catalogs are large but miss the bugs E-02 names. Alzahrani and Vahid's survey of 47 publications identifies 166 logic errors in 11 categories ([ASEE PEER](https://peer.asee.org/36814)), and logic errors persist after syntax errors fade over a course ([arXiv 2502.14438](https://www.arxiv.org/pdf/2502.14438)), yet progmiscon.org's 30 curated Python misconceptions include nothing on print-versus-return, range bounds or integer division ([progmiscon](https://progmiscon.org/misconceptions/Python/)). CodeQuest therefore needs its own detectors built on Python's `ast` module ([Python ast docs](https://docs.python.org/3/library/ast.html)). Six cover the commonest function-exercise failures: a target function that prints but never returns; a risky operation on a parameter (`p[0]`, `max(p)`, dividing by `len(p)`) with no earlier empty guard; `range(len(x)-1)`-style bounds; a builtin such as `sum` or `list` reassigned; recursion without a base case that runs first; and a loop that mutates the list it iterates.

Static patterns alone accuse correct code (some `range(len(x)-1)` loops are right, for pairwise comparisons), so the fusion rule matters more than any single detector. Record a recurring-mistake event only when the runtime signature is unambiguous, as with `RecursionError` or "'int' object is not callable", or when a static hit and a runtime signal of the same category agree, such as a missing empty guard plus failures on the `empty`-tagged hidden tests. A static-only hit becomes a soft "consider…" note that leaves the learner model untouched.

Two heavier techniques are worth knowing and mostly worth deferring. Spectrum-based fault localization (SBFL) ranks lines by how often failing tests execute them, but even on Defects4J's 357 real faults the best formulas placed the faulty statement in the top five only **31%** of the time, and stack traces beat it for crashes ([Zou et al.](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/1803.09939)); it also performs poorly on student programs ([IEICE Transactions](https://global.ieice.org/en_transactions/information/10.1587/transinf.2023EDP7164/_f)). Heatmaps did rank the defective method among the top three 90% of the time on introductory Java projects with larger test suites ([VTechWorks](https://vtechworks.lib.vt.edu/handle/10919/96008)), but in a ten-line Python function nearly every line runs on every test and most lines tie, while an AST detector points straight at the faulty `range`. SBFL is therefore at most a P1 "look here" heatmap. Differential property-based testing earns its place sooner: Hypothesis (MPL-2.0, Python 3.10+) can assert that the learner's function matches the reference on generated inputs and shrink any failure to a minimal counterexample ([Hypothesis on PyPI](https://pypi.org/project/hypothesis/); [Hypothesis API reference](https://hypothesis.readthedocs.io/en/latest/reference/api.html)). Inside the sandbox it should run with no deadline, no example database and a fixed seed, filter out hidden-test inputs, and draw values from a domain disjoint from the hidden tests. Degenerate witnesses such as `[]` will often coincide with an `empty` hidden test, so they should appear only from the second hint rung.

### Four hint rungs, no bottom-out rung, and an executable leak check

Intelligent-tutoring research supplies both the ladder and its failure mode. The Andes physics tutor escalates from a pointing hint to a teaching hint to a "bottom-out" hint that says what to enter ([VanLehn slides](https://companion.ict.usc.edu/talks/VanLehn%20ILLC-ICT%203Oct08%20-%20Interaction%20Plateau.pdf)); in a Geometry Cognitive Tutor study students abused on-demand help on **36% of actions**, or 14% when rapid repeated requests count once, typically by clicking straight through to the bottom-out hint ([LearnLab](https://learnlab.org/research/wiki/index.php/Help_abuse)); and requesting hints immediately on hitting difficulty is associated with poorer learning ([An et al.](https://www.cs.cmu.edu/afs/cs.cmu.edu/Web/People/bmclaren/pubs/AnEtAl-RevisitingTheHintButton-LAK2026.pdf)). Novices also need concrete lower rungs: high-level natural-language hints alone were often unhelpful for next-step and syntax problems ([Xiao et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2404.02213)), and next-step code hints improved later performance only when paired with explanations and self-explanation prompts ([Marwan et al.](https://isnap.csc.ncsu.edu/home/public/publication/marwan-2019-icer)).

E-02's rule that no hint may reveal "the complete solution, including AI-generated hints" turns this into four rungs and no fifth. The first says *where*, naming a two-to-four-line region from a detector or a diff against the reference. The second says *what*, giving the mistake category plus a counterexample that is not a hidden test. The third *teaches* the concept through a worked example on a different mini-problem, with a one-line self-explanation prompt. The fourth shows the *shape* of the single faulty construct with blanks, such as `for i in range(___, ___):`. Specificity becomes monotone by construction if each rung reveals a set of facts from a fixed vocabulary (concept, region, category, counterexample, skeleton) that strictly contains the previous rung's set. The next rung unlocks only after a changed resubmission or a short cooldown, success after the third or fourth rung counts as a failure for mastery, and rapid-fire requests are logged as help abuse. An exam mode that withholds hints and AI, as Codeforces and AtCoder already require in rated contests ([Codeforces blog](https://codeforces.com/blog/entry/133941); [AtCoder rules](https://info.atcoder.jp/entry/llm-rules-en)), gives learners an honest readiness score.

The leak guard runs its cheapest checks first, because no validated text-similarity threshold for hint leakage exists. It rejects any hint containing a code block or a fragment that `ast.parse` accepts as a non-trivial statement, and any hint containing a hidden-test literal or a member of an automatically derived avoid set (identifiers, literals and calls present in the reference solution but absent from the starter code and statement), an idea adapted from CodeHelp's instructor avoid sets ([CodeHelp](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.06921)). Then comes the decisive executable check: splice any surviving code-like fragment into the learner's program, run the hidden tests, and treat a full pass as a leak. That mirrors how Zhao and colleagues score code leakage, by running official tests on code extracted from the tutor's reply ([Zhao et al.](https://arxiv.org/html/2604.18660v1)), and how PyFiXV and its successors validate feedback by execution before showing it ([Phung et al. 2023](https://arxiv.org/abs/2302.04662); [Phung et al. 2024](https://arxiv.org/abs/2310.03780)). Running the same guard over every authored template as a pytest suite catches authoring slips before the judges do.

### Hand-set knowledge tracing turns repeated bugs into the next challenge

Learner modeling needs neither training data nor deep learning. Khajah, Lindsey and Mozer showed that Bayesian Knowledge Tracing (BKT) extended for forgetting and individual ability performs indistinguishably from deep knowledge tracing ([Khajah et al.](https://arxiv.org/pdf/1604.02416)), and the BKT update is a few lines of arithmetic ([pyBKT paper](https://ar5iv.labs.arxiv.org/html/2105.00385)). With hand-set parameters (prior 0.3, learning 0.15, guess 0.2, slip 0.1), a learner passes a 0.95 mastery threshold after three consecutive clean successes, responsive enough for a demo and easy to explain; 0.95 is a common convention rather than a verified standard, so the interface should show bands (learning, almost there, mastered) instead of decimals. Use the first submission per challenge as the main observation, count the share of a concept's tagged tests passed as partial evidence, and discount success that followed high hint rungs. For choosing what comes next, Pelánek's Elo variant keeps one skill per learner and concept and one difficulty per challenge, replacing a fixed step size with the uncertainty function U(n) = 1/(1 + 0.05n) ([Pelánek 2016](https://www.fi.muni.cz/~xpelanek/publications/CAE-elo.pdf)). A randomized experiment with about 37,000 learners found that easy items held learners best at first while harder items improved learning and return rates ([Papoušek et al.](https://www.fi.muni.cz/~xpelanek/publications/its-target-difficulty.pdf)), which argues for an easy first session followed by a predicted success of roughly 0.65–0.75.

The adaptation E-02 demands comes from a separate layer of misconception counters fed by the fusion rule. Each counter decays by 0.8 per submission; a mistake is recurring once it fires in two of the last five submissions; and the next slot then goes to a challenge whose hidden tests stress that edge or to a debugging drill seeded with the same bug. Mutation makes those drills deterministic. A hand-written `ast.NodeTransformer` with about ten operators can label each operator with a misconception (`<` to `<=` for a boundary off-by-one, deleting the `if not xs:` guard for unhandled empty input, `return` to `print`, moving an accumulator inside the loop), which general tools such as mutmut do not do, and mutmut also needs WSL on Windows ([mutmut](https://github.com/boxed/mutmut)). Equivalent mutants are the main reliability problem, since compiler-equivalence checks catch only about 30% of them ([Papadakis et al.](https://orbilu.uni.lu/handle/10993/20289)), so keep only mutants that some input distinguishes from the reference. BugSpotter showed that generated debugging exercises, in which students first write a failing test, perform comparably to instructor-written ones ([BugSpotter](https://arxiv.org/abs/2411.14303)); HackerRank's Zig Zag Sequence adds a cheap constraint that forces localization, letting learners "modify at most three lines" ([HackerRank](https://www.hackerrank.com/challenges/one-week-preparation-kit-zig-zag-sequence/problem)); and the motivation is real, since good programmers are not always good debuggers ([Fitzgerald et al.](https://research.aber.ac.uk/en/publications/debugging-finding-fixing-and-flailing-a-multi-institutional-study/)). No product reviewed builds a drill from the learner's own recurring bug class. A full step-through tracer, modeled on Python Tutor's 1,000-step cap ([pg_logger.py](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/dynl04d/OnlinePythonTutor/master/v5-unity/pg_logger.py)), is a stretch goal; visualization research finds that how learners engage matters more than what is shown ([Hundhausen et al.](https://www.academia.edu/15636659/A_Meta_Study_of_Algorithm_Visualization_Effectiveness)), so a "predict the next value" checkpoint beats passive playback.

| Technique | E-02 clause served | Build hours (estimate) | Priority |
|---|---|---|---|
| Exception templates with crash-frame variables | Plain-language explanation | 5–7 | P0 |
| Output-shape classifier (about 15 rules) | Explanation; mistake evidence | 3–4 | P0 |
| Six AST detectors with test-tag fusion | Recurring mistakes | about 4 | P0 |

The hours are single-developer engineering estimates, not measurements. The P0 core comes to roughly 25–36 person-hours, which fits one backend owner plus part of a second across 24 hours if work starts with the templates and the classifier. The demo moment follows directly: a seeded learner fails the empty-list hidden test twice, the counter fires, the next challenge swaps to one whose hidden tests probe empty inputs (or to a drill seeded with a deleted guard), and the concept bar moves.

## A throwaway container per test phase beats every free judge

### The free judges teams relied on are closed, capped or compromised

The shortcuts student teams once reached for no longer hold. Judge0's newest release is still **v1.13.1 from April 2024**, GPL-3.0, and its documented install forces a cgroup v1 host through a GRUB flag ([Judge0 changelog](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/judge0/judge0/master/CHANGELOG.md)), while systemd 258 removed cgroup v1 entirely ([Linuxiac](https://linuxiac.com/systemd-258-drops-cgroup-v1-raises-kernel-baseline-to-5-4/)) and current isolate supports only cgroup v2 ([isolate manual](https://www.ucw.cz/isolate/isolate.1.html)). Its 2024 escapes ran through host-side glue rather than the sandbox itself: CVE-2024-28185 (CVSS 10.0) wrote outside the sandbox through an unaccounted symlink, CVE-2024-28189 (CVSS 10.0) bypassed the patch through a symlink-following `chown`, and CVE-2024-29021 (CVSS 9.1) let sandboxed code with network access reach the internal PostgreSQL service and run commands as root ([The Hacker News](https://thehackernews.com/2024/04/sandbox-escape-vulnerabilities-in.html); [Tanto Security](https://tantosec.com/blog/judge0/)). Piston's public API "is no longer freely available to the public (as of Feb 15, 2026)", and its token policy excludes temporary projects and school assignments ([Piston README](https://github.com/engineer-man/piston)); Judge0 CE's free tier allows about 50 submissions a day ([Judge0 CE](https://judge0.com/ce)), which a red-team session would exhaust in minutes.

Python-level controls are not boundaries either. The Python documentation says audit hooks "are not suitable for implementing a 'sandbox'" because "malicious code can trivially disable or bypass" them ([Python sys docs](https://docs.python.org/3/library/sys.html)); RestrictedPython calls itself "not sufficient as a sandbox environment" ([RestrictedPython](https://restrictedpython.readthedocs.io/en/latest/idea.html)); and `resource.setrlimit` and Windows Job Objects cap time and memory but block neither file reads nor network access ([Python resource docs](https://docs.python.org/3/library/resource.html); [Microsoft Learn](https://learn.microsoft.com/en-us/windows/win32/procthread/job-objects)). E-02 accepts "a constrained local test runner" only when full sandboxing "is not feasible", so such a runner should be labelled dev mode and switched off in the deployed demo, with audit hooks kept inside the real sandbox as tripwires that turn blocked actions into friendly error messages.

### Hardened Docker on a VM, a microVM fallback and Pyodide for speed

The robust primary is a fresh, hardened Docker container per submission phase on a small Linux VM. Each flag is standard and demo-able: `--network none`, a read-only root with a 16 MB `noexec` tmpfs, `--memory 256m` with `--memory-swap` set equal so swap is off, one CPU, `--pids-limit 32`, `--cap-drop ALL`, `no-new-privileges`, Docker's default seccomp profile, the unprivileged `nobody` user, and `--init` to reap orphans ([Docker resource constraints](https://docs.docker.com/engine/containers/resource_constraints/); [docker run reference](https://docs.docker.com/reference/cli/docker/container/run/)). gVisor's `runsc` runtime adds a userspace kernel, and its default systrap platform works inside VMs without nested virtualization ([gVisor platforms](https://gvisor.dev/docs/architecture_guide/platforms/)). Wall-clock limits and output caps must be enforced from outside, by a host process that reads bounded pipes and kills the container, because CPU rlimits ignore sleeping code. The runner VM should hold no secrets, with the app and database hosted elsewhere, and the same image runs on the team's Windows laptops through Docker Desktop, so development and production behave alike.

Where the runner lives matters. Render will not run privileged containers ([Render community](https://community.render.com/t/run-docker-container-in-privileged-mode/1814)) and Railway's service containers lack the `unshare` support sandboxes need ([Railway Station](https://station.railway.com/questions/does-railway-support-unprivileged-bubble-dfd8911e)), so those hosts can serve the web app but not the runner. Oracle's Always Free tier now offers the equivalent of **2 OCPUs and 12 GB** on Ampere ARM, with frequent "out of host capacity" errors ([Oracle docs](https://docs.oracle.com/en-us/iaas/Content/FreeTier/freetier_topic-Always_Free_Resources.htm)), and Ubuntu 24.04's AppArmor blocks unprivileged user namespaces by default, which breaks unprivileged bubblewrap and nsjail but not Docker ([Full Disclosure](https://seclists.org/fulldisclosure/2025/Mar/8)). If no VM is running by T+02:00, the managed fallback becomes primary behind the same runner interface. E2B offers a one-time $100 credit with no card and up to 20 concurrent sandboxes ([E2B pricing](https://e2b.dev/pricing)); Modal gives $30 a month in credits and runs sandboxes on gVisor by default ([Modal pricing](https://modal.com/pricing); [Modal Sandboxes](https://modal.com/docs/guide/sandboxes)); and Vercel Sandbox's Hobby plan includes 5 active CPU-hours, 5,000 sandbox creations a month and a deny-all policy that also blocks DNS ([Vercel Sandbox pricing](https://vercel.com/docs/vercel-sandbox/pricing); [Vercel firewall docs](https://vercel.com/docs/sandbox/concepts/firewall)). At E2B's per-second prices a ten-second run on one vCPU and 1 GiB costs about $0.0002, so concurrency limits, not credit, are what bind. **Internet access is on by default in E2B and Modal**, so the code must assert `allow_internet_access=False` or `block_network=True`, and a network probe belongs in the test suite ([E2B docs](https://docs.e2b.dev/sandbox/internet-access); [Modal networking](https://modal.com/docs/guide/sandbox-networking)).

For responsiveness, Pyodide 314.x, which ships Python 3.14, can run visible tests in a Web Worker ([Pyodide changelog](https://pyodide.org/en/stable/project/changelog.html)), with interrupts through a SharedArrayBuffer (which requires cross-origin isolation) and `worker.terminate()` as the guaranteed hard kill ([Pyodide docs](https://pyodide.org/en/stable/usage/keyboard-interrupts.html)). Its security boundary is the learner's own browser tab, so it must never receive hidden tests, and the server re-judges everything on Submit.

### Hidden tests leak through design flaws, not sandbox escapes

A perfect sandbox still leaks hidden tests if the pipeline around it is careless. Each test should run as a fresh `python -I` process fed only its own input on stdin. Expected outputs and checkers should never enter the sandbox; results should leave as JSON primitives and be compared on the host, which defeats an object whose `__eq__` always returns True or a printed `{"status":"passed"}`. Hidden tests should run in a separate container after the visible ones, because code could otherwise stash a hidden input in `/tmp` and print it during a visible test, and their stdout, stderr and exception messages should never reach the client. The verdict itself is an oracle: four outcome classes leak up to two bits per hidden test per submission, so ten hidden tests at 30 submissions an hour leak about 600 bits an hour, enough to reconstruct small inputs. That argues for per-user limits of about six submissions a minute and a hundred a day, and for hiding per-test timings. The LLM must never see hidden tests or reference solutions, which makes prompt injection through code comments pointless. A survey of online-judge security lists covert channels and bypassed time measurement among the classic attack classes ([MDPI Electronics](https://www.mdpi.com/2079-9292/12/14/3018)), and Judge0's SSRF shows why the network must be absent rather than filtered ([Tanto Security](https://tantosec.com/blog/judge0/)).

| Resource | Starting limit | Verdict when exceeded |
|---|---|---|
| Wall clock per test | 2 s, hard kill at 2.5 s | Timeout |
| CPU time per test | 2 s soft, 3 s hard | Timeout |
| Whole submission | 20 s | Unrun tests reported as Timeout |
| Memory | 256 MiB, swap off | Runtime error (memory limit) |
| Processes | 32 per container | Runtime error or Timeout |
| Captured stdout | 64 KiB, then kill | Wrong output (output limit exceeded) |
| Network | None | Runtime error |
| Submission rate | 6 a minute, 100 a day per user | HTTP 429 |

These are reasoned defaults to tune on the chosen VM, not benchmarks. The surrounding system stays small. Production judges such as CMS and DOMjudge share one shape, a queue feeding stateless workers that each run sandboxed per-test processes, with judge daemons pinned to dedicated cores ([CMS docs](https://cms.readthedocs.io/en/latest/Introduction.html); [DOMjudge docs](https://www.domjudge.org/docs/manual/main/install-judgehost.html)); at hackathon scale that becomes an `asyncio` semaphore sized to the runner's vCPUs, an idempotency key per submission, and retries only for infrastructure failures, never for learner-caused errors. A 25-program red-team suite run as pytest covers reading `/etc/passwd` and the environment, sockets to the cloud metadata address, fork and memory bombs, `time.sleep` to dodge CPU limits, orphaned children, symlink traps, verdict forgery and hidden-input printing; it doubles as a live demo segment and, once recorded, as a fallback if the venue network fails.

## The four extras pay off only as feeds into one mistake model

Every extra already exists as a standalone product, so none is novel on its own; each becomes defensible only when it writes to or reads from the same mistake profile and concept model. The build order that follows is the core loop first, then the mistake detector and skill map, then deployment with seeded demo data, and only then the extras in the order below, all before the feature freeze.

| Extra | Feasibility in 24 hours | Main risk | Minimum credible scope | Effort (estimate) |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| Skill map and personalized LeetCode roadmap | High: reuses the learner model and open datasets | Collecting more personal data than the purpose needs | Two-layer concept map, Elo-based planner, six-to-eight-question onboarding | Low on top of the core; the week planner is about 30 lines |
| Unified portfolio | High for CodeQuest evidence plus LeetCode and Codeforces imports | Unofficial LeetCode endpoint; crawler fragility | "Verified on CodeQuest" evidence block plus cached, badged imports | Moderate; the tag map alone takes 1–2 hours |
| LeetCode companion extension | Medium as a click-triggered panel; low as an automatic interceptor | Terms of service, contest rules, UI drift | Unpacked MV3 panel with three buttons | 4–6 hours with prior MV3 experience |
| Animated typing-speed trainer | Technically easy, strategically weak | Off-brief, commodity, copyleft references | 60–90-second syntax warm-up, or cut | 2–4 hours for the warm-up; 10–12 for a full MVP |

### A roadmap needs goals and hours, not identity

"Built from your personal info" should mean a learner's goal, schedule and demonstrated performance, not identity data. Comparable products ask very little: Grind 75 needs only weeks and hours per week ([Grind 75 logic](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/grind75/_next/static/chunks/8a45c2bbae4d39ea.js)); Duolingo's placement test starts from novice and re-estimates after every answer ([Duolingo blog](https://blog.duolingo.com/partial-credit-improvements-to-duolingos-placement-test/)); and ALEKS places students with 30 or fewer adaptive questions ([McGraw Hill](https://www.mheducation.com/support/aleks-support-center/knowledge/what-is-a-knowledge-check.html)). An NIH adaptive test stopped at a standard-error threshold with a 12-item cap, and fixing its length at eight or fewer items notably increased the share of unreliable assessments ([PMC](https://www.ncbi.nlm.nih.gov/pmc/articles/PMC11951945/)), so a placement check of 8–12 short "predict the output" or "spot the bug" items is the right size, although no study has yet validated such micro-items as proxies for problem-solving skill. India's DPDP Act limits consent to data "necessary for such specified purpose", defines a child as anyone under 18, requires verifiable parental consent, and bars "tracking or behavioural monitoring of children" ([DPDP Act](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2024/06/2bf1f0e9f04e6fb4f8fef35e82c42aa5.pdf)), which an adaptive mistake profile arguably is. The Rules' notice, security and children's duties take effect 18 months after their November 2025 publication, around May 2027, and they expressly allow processing to confirm that a user is not a child ([DPDP Rules](https://www.meity.gov.in/static/uploads/2025/11/53450e6e5dc0bfa85ebd78686cadad39.pdf)); penalties for breaching children's obligations reach ₹200 crore ([PIB explainer](https://static.pib.gov.in/WriteReadData/specificdocs/documents/2025/nov/doc20251117695301.pdf)). Because some first-year students are still 17, the safe design is an 18+ gate with an in-browser guest mode for younger users, plus six to eight questions: goal (service-company placement, product-company placement, FAANG-level interviews, competitive programming or fundamentals), target weeks, hours per week, self-described level, optional topic comfort and whether the learner has LeetCode Premium. Name, phone, date of birth, college, CGPA, résumé and named target companies are excessive for the purpose, and an itemised notice with delete and export buttons costs little.

The engine can be assembled from open data. NeetCode's roadmap is an explicit prerequisite graph of **18 topics and 21 edges** recoverable from its site code ([NeetCode roadmap chunk](https://neetcode.io/6252.838a46ce34355e13.js)), to which about six Python-fundamentals nodes attach upstream. The skill map should have two layers, Python language concepts evidenced only by CodeQuest runs and algorithmic topics evidenced by both CodeQuest and imports, linked by edges such as recursion feeding backtracking and dynamic programming, with verified CodeQuest evidence outweighing self-reported external results. Grind's generator, recovered from its production bundle, sorts 169 questions by priority and subtracts 1.96 times each recommended duration from a budget of 60 × hours × weeks minutes; its 8-week, 8-hour default reproduces exactly the top 75 ([Grind 75 logic](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/grind75/_next/static/chunks/8a45c2bbae4d39ea.js); [Grind 75 data](https://www.techinterviewhandbook.org/grind75/_next/static/chunks/c5797dfbd9aea01f.js)). zerotrac's ratings cover 2,608 contest problems under MIT and update weekly ([zerotrac](https://github.com/zerotrac/leetcode_problem_rating)), but they rate none of Blind 75, 11% of NeetCode 150 and 8% of Grind 169, so unrated LeetCode problems need label priors; among rated NeetCode-site problems the medians are 1247 for Easy, 1635 for Medium and 2084 for Hard ([zerotrac data](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/zerotrac/leetcode_problem_rating/main/data.json); [NeetCode bundle](https://neetcode.io/main.f39af0c52a4e9fb5.js)). On that Elo-400 scale Pelánek's step size becomes K(n) = 174/(1 + 0.05n). Calibrate difficulty only for CodeQuest's own challenges and hold external problems fixed, because Elo ratings fail to converge when items are chosen adaptively from current ratings ([Tilburg University](https://research.tilburguniversity.edu/en/publications/keeping-elo-alive-evaluating-and-improving-measurement-properties/)).

The "what next" score should blend closeness to the target success rate, readiness on the prerequisite fringe, a trap term that pulls in problems exercising the learner's recurring mistakes, review due-ness from FSRS at 90% desired retention ([py-fsrs](https://github.com/open-spaced-repetition/py-fsrs)) and goal weight, and should interleave topics after two or three blocked items. Interleaving lifted delayed test scores from 38% to 72% in a seventh-grade math study ([Rohrer et al.](https://link.springer.com/article/10.3758/s13423-014-0588-3)), although a year-long trial found no effect on a cumulative assessment ([EdWorkingPapers](https://edworkingpapers.com/ai23-876)) and evidence for programming is thin. Licensing decides the data: NeetCode's GitHub problem data is MIT ([NeetCode license](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/neetcode-gh/leetcode/main/LICENSE)), Sean Prashad's pattern list with company frequencies is CC BY-NC 4.0 ([LeetCode Patterns license](https://raw.githubusercontent.com/seanprashad/leetcode-patterns/main/LICENSE)), and the popular company-wise lists are unlicensed snapshots of LeetCode Premium data, one with about 31,000 GitHub stars ([GitHub API](https://api.github.com/repos/liquidslr/leetcode-company-wise-problems)). Offer goal types instead of named companies, store only slugs, titles and links, and seed three personas so judges can watch the plan re-pack after a mistake recurs.

### The portfolio should lead with evidence no aggregator has

Only Codeforces and GitHub offer real official APIs. Codeforces answers anonymous calls "at most 1 time per two seconds", and `user.status` returns every submission's verdict, passed-test count and problem tags; its problem set holds 11,152 rated problems under 38 tags ([Codeforces API help](https://codeforces.com/apiHelp); [Codeforces problemset](https://codeforces.com/api/problemset.problems)). LeetCode's undocumented GraphQL endpoint answered anonymous queries on 11 October 2026 for solved counts, per-tag counts already grouped into fundamental, intermediate and advanced tiers, contest rating, the activity calendar and the last 20 submissions including failed verdicts, but it sends no CORS headers, so a website must proxy it server-side, and field names drift: a query for `skillStats` now fails with a schema error ([LeetCode GraphQL](https://leetcode.com/graphql)). CodeChef's official API returned HTTP 405 and a community proxy had been disabled ([api.codechef.com](https://api.codechef.com/); [codechef-api.vercel.app](https://codechef-api.vercel.app/handle/gennady.korotkevich)), and HackerRank and GeeksforGeeks are reachable only through internal endpoints or scraping.

The portfolio should therefore lead with a **"Verified on CodeQuest"** block, a concept-mastery grid whose cells link to evidence (hidden tests passed, attempts, hint rungs used, mistakes fixed) plus a "recurring mistakes, resolved" timeline, and show LeetCode and Codeforces imports below it, cached for 24 hours, badged as unverified and backed by a seeded snapshot so a failed fetch never blocks rendering. A LeetCode or Codeforces handle should be requested only in this flow, optionally and with a stated purpose. A static map of about 40 rows onto CodeQuest's concepts handles the two vocabularies (75 LeetCode tags, 38 Codeforces tags), with care for false friends: Codeforces `hashing` means string hashing, not hash tables, and `matrices` means linear algebra, not grid traversal. The Apache-2.0 TACO dataset, which relabels problems from ten platforms with unified algorithm tags, can validate the map later ([TACO](https://arxiv.org/pdf/2312.14852)). CodeChef, HackerRank, GeeksforGeeks and leetcode.cn, which served a Cloudflare challenge instead of JSON, stay out of the 24-hour scope. The pitch writes itself: other trackers count what you solved; CodeQuest shows how you learned.

### The LeetCode companion survives only as a thin, user-triggered panel

The legal and technical ground is shaky. LeetCode's terms forbid "crawling," "scraping," or "spidering" and reverse engineering ([LeetCode ToS snapshot](http://web.archive.org/web/20260915150045/https://leetcode.com/terms/)); its robots.txt disallows `/graphql` ([robots.txt](https://leetcode.com/robots.txt)); and, per its announced contest rules, entering problem statements or code into external assistance tools during a contest is a violation, punished by a score reset and one-month ban for a first offence and permanent deactivation for a second ([LeetCode Discuss](https://leetcode.com/discuss/general-discussion/951105/New-Contest-Rule-)). Existing extensions show both the technique and its fragility: LeetHub-3.0 monkey-patches `window.fetch` in the page's main world to catch submission IDs, then fetches the code through GraphQL with the user's own cookies ([LeetHub-3.0 interceptor](https://github.com/raphaelheinz/LeetHub-3.0/blob/main/src/js/interceptor.js)), and the maintained LeetHub forks depend on generated CSS classes and DOM locators that change with LeetCode's interface. The market prefers evidence to advice: LeetHub v2, which syncs solutions to GitHub, has **70,000 users** ([Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/mhanfgfagplhgemhjfeolkkdidbakocm)), while the largest AI-hint extension found, LeetBuddy, has 2,000 ([Chrome Web Store](https://chromewebstore.google.com/detail/nlemdecocfoaimdbfgpilfgdmcllhphn)). Manifest V3 adds constraints: service workers die after 30 seconds of inactivity or on a fetch longer than 30 seconds ([Chrome service-worker lifecycle](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/extensions/develop/concepts/service-workers/lifecycle)), and the store forbids remotely hosted logic and limits data use to a disclosed single purpose ([CWS MV3 requirements](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/program-policies/mv3-requirements); [CWS Limited Use](https://developer.chrome.com/docs/webstore/program-policies/limited-use)).

The feasible version is a load-unpacked panel that acts only when clicked. A main-world script reads the editor through Monaco's `getModels()` ([Monaco typings](https://unpkg.com/monaco-editor@0.52.2/esm/vs/editor/editor.api.d.ts)), which LeetCode exposes as `window.monaco` to page-context scripts ([leetcode-vim notes](https://zff.dev/CharlieBacon/leetcode-vim)), and the panel offers three buttons. "Check my approach" takes a one-to-three-sentence plan and matches its named techniques deterministically against the problem's topic tags and CodeQuest's concept map, optionally adding a nano-model classification into on-track, partially, off-track or unclear plus one question, never code. "Hint" serves CodeQuest's own concept-level ladder, never LeetCode's copyrighted hints. "Analyze my code" runs the same Python AST detectors the core uses, so "checking your solution" means static mistake detection, not running LeetCode's hidden tests. Results sync to the learner's profile as external, self-reported evidence; the panel disables itself on contest pages; no code leaves the browser without a click; and a recorded video covers the demo if LeetCode's UI or Cloudflare breaks it live. Automatic verdict capture and store publication are not worth the risk in 24 hours.

### The typing trainer is off-brief unless it becomes remediation

E-02 never mentions typing, and its examples of recurring mistakes, off-by-one errors and unhandled empty inputs, are logic errors. The evidence for typing practice is correlational: keystroke latency at symbol-boundary key pairs correlated with programming scores at about r = 0.5 with "substantial scatter", and its authors concluded "these metrics are not sufficient to measure programming performance" ([Thomas et al. 2005](https://cms.simula.no/sites/default/files/publications/Thomas.2005.1.pdf)); students who chose to do NCSU's code-retyping exercises earned higher grades, but they selected themselves ([NCSU TYPOS](https://research.csc.ncsu.edu/arglab/projects/typos/)); and Mark Seemann's arithmetic puts real programming output near 27 characters a minute ([ploeh blog](https://blog.ploeh.dk/2018/09/17/typing-is-not-a-programming-bottleneck/)). The honest case is narrower: mismatched brackets and quotes were the most frequent errors in 37 million novice Java compilations ([It Will Never Work in Theory](https://neverworkintheory.org/2016/06/09/novice-programming-mistakes.html)), so short, accuracy-first drills on syntax have some value.

A full animated trainer with a smooth caret, per-symbol statistics and a personal-best ghost is an estimated 10–12 hours, a third to half of the front-end owner's productive time, and the best references, Monkeytype (GPL-3.0) and keybr (AGPL-3.0), cannot be forked without copyleft obligations ([Monkeytype](https://github.com/monkeytypegame/monkeytype); [keybr](https://github.com/aradzie/keybr.com)). If at least four hours remain before the feature freeze, the defensible version is a 60–90-second "syntax warm-up" triggered when a syntax category such as a missing colon, an unclosed bracket or `=` versus `==` recurs, built from the learner's own accepted code and never from unsolved challenges, scored on accuracy on syntax-critical characters, with a personal pace caret set at recent median speed plus 5% only after accuracy reaches 95%. Timing should use the monotonic `performance.now()` clock ([MDN](https://developer.mozilla.org/en-US/docs/Web/API/Performance/now)), and only per-drill aggregates should be stored, because typing rhythms identify individuals even after names are removed ([Leinonen et al.](https://researchportal.tuni.fi/en/publications/preventing-keystroke-based-identification-in-open-data-sets/)) and GDPR counts behavioural identifiers as biometric data ([GDPR Art. 4](https://gdpr-info.eu/art-4-gdpr/)). Otherwise the trainer belongs on a roadmap slide.

## Two and a half dollars buys ten thousand calls but not reliability

At October 2026 list prices the credit is ample for the narrow job the LLM should do ([OpenAI API pricing](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/pricing)). The table assumes a 1,500-token prompt and a 250-token reply.

| Model | Input / output per 1M tokens | Cost per call | Calls for $2.50 |
|---|---|---|---|
| gpt-5-nano | $0.05 / $0.40 | $0.000175 | about 14,300 |
| gpt-4.1-nano | $0.10 / $0.40 | $0.000250 | about 10,000 |
| gpt-6-luna | $0.10 / $0.50 | $0.000275 | about 9,100 |
| gpt-4o-mini | $0.15 / $0.60 | $0.000375 | about 6,700 |
| gpt-5-mini | $0.25 / $2.00 | $0.000875 | about 2,900 |

Counts halve with a second judging call or about 500 hidden reasoning tokens per call, since reasoning tokens are billed as output and count toward `max_output_tokens` ([OpenAI reasoning guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/reasoning)). For scale, CS50 paid about $0.05 per GPT-4 prompt in 2023 ([CS50 paper](https://cs.harvard.edu/malan/publications/V1fp0567-liu.pdf)), so the same $2.50 would then have bought 50 prompts. **Money is no longer the constraint**; a quota error, a slow reply or a leaky hint in the middle of the demo is. Research hint pipelines took 15–34 seconds per hint even on API models ([Hints-In-Browser](https://arxiv.org/html/2406.05053v2)), so a hard timeout of about four seconds with a template fallback is essential.

Spending controls need care because alerts do not stop spending. OpenAI's spend alerts "do not enforce a cap", while a hard project limit returns HTTP 429 with `project_spend_limit_exceeded`, though enforcement "is not instantaneous" ([OpenAI spend limits](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/spend-limits)). Splitting the credit into a dev project capped near $1.00 and a demo project near $1.25, using per-project limits ([OpenAI production best practices](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/production-best-practices)), stops testing from draining the demo and turns the 429 into a clean fallback trigger. Ten to twenty test calls that read the reasoning-token count should decide between gpt-4.1-nano for predictability and gpt-5-nano for price, and every call should set `max_output_tokens`. Structured Outputs guarantee adherence to a JSON schema and return refusals in a separate field ([OpenAI Structured Outputs](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/structured-outputs)), so a schema with an enum hint level and a length-capped text field ensures free-form model text never reaches a learner unchecked. A stable prompt prefix of at least 1,024 tokens qualifies for caching discounts of up to 95% on GPT-5.6 and later models ([OpenAI prompt caching](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/prompt-caching)), and the Batch API's 50% discount, with results within 24 hours ([OpenAI Batch guide](https://developers.openai.com/api/docs/guides/batch)), suits pre-generating guarded rephrasings for every seeded challenge well before judging. The expiry date of prepaid credit could not be verified, so check the billing dashboard rather than assume the balance survives the event.

Every provider should sit behind the same OpenAI SDK. Gemini exposes an OpenAI-compatible endpoint and lists a free tier for its Flash and Flash-Lite models, but free-tier content is used to improve Google's products, so only synthetic or anonymised code should go there ([Gemini OpenAI compatibility](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/openai); [Gemini pricing](https://ai.google.dev/gemini-api/docs/pricing)). Groq is also OpenAI-compatible, and its published table, labelled as Developer-plan limits, lists gpt-oss-20b at 30 requests a minute, 1,000 a day and 200,000 tokens a day, so the free plan's actual quota needs checking in the console at T+00:00 ([Groq rate limits](https://console.groq.com/docs/rate-limits); [Groq OpenAI compatibility](https://console.groq.com/docs/openai)). OpenRouter's free models stop at 50 requests a day for accounts that have bought fewer than 10 credits ([OpenRouter limits](https://openrouter.ai/docs/api-reference/limits)). The last link is always the deterministic template plus cached rephrasings, reached on any 429 or 402, an exhausted-credit error, a timeout or a guard rejection. Local models make poor primaries: Philip Guo found 1B and 3B models frequently wrong and moved Python Tutor's AI chat to a hosted 70B model, serving about 2,000 queries a day for under $1 ([Guo case study](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10618667)).

Safety depends on what the model may see and say. The key lives only in the backend's `.env`, never in the browser bundle, the extension (which relays through the backend anyway) or the sandbox environment, which the red-team suite inspects. Prompts carry a facts object (exception type, line, failing-test category and the selected template) and never hidden tests or reference solutions, and every rephrase must still name the same exception, line and category before it is shown. Learner code is untrusted input: strip comments and docstrings and truncate string literals before it enters a prompt, since injection through ordinary-looking submissions already works against LLM graders ([Li et al.](https://arxiv.org/abs/2606.03090)), and send detected override phrases straight to the template rather than ending the session. One-shot buttons ("Explain my error", "Ask me a question") replace open chat, following CodeHelp's one-shot design and Python Tutor's one-chat-per-minute throttle ([CodeHelp](https://arxiv.org/abs/2308.06921); [Guo case study](https://par.nsf.gov/servlets/purl/10618667)). Off-the-shelf guard models are no substitute for this pipeline: none of those tested on 8,000 classroom prompts exceeded 0.65 F1, against 0.93 for a small fine-tuned classifier ([CodeGuard](https://arxiv.org/abs/2602.02509)), and NeMo Guardrails flagged 16% of legitimate requests ([Maiorano](https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.06669)). The LLM's useful job is to rephrase a correct diagnosis, pose one Socratic question and classify the extension's approach descriptions; it never authors hints at runtime and never decides a verdict.

## Conclusion

E-02 reads like a brief for an AI tutor, but every clause a judge can test (per-test outcomes, hidden-test secrecy, hints that grow more specific without completing the solution, adaptation to recurring mistakes, concept tracking) is a deterministic property, and the market's most expensive feature, metered AI help, is its least defensible one. The cheapest ingredients (tags on hidden tests, a handful of AST detectors and a decaying counter per mistake class) produce the thing no platform ships: a learner model fed by verified evidence of what someone keeps getting wrong. That model also turns the extras from clones of Codolio, LeetHub, NeetCode and Monkeytype into one product, in which the extension is an evidence intake, the portfolio a display of verified evidence, the roadmap a consumer of the mistake profile and the typing drill a remediation step. The economics have flipped as well: when CS50 paid a nickel a prompt, budget decided what an AI tutor could do, while at nano-model prices the binding constraints are leakage, latency and quota failures, and architecture rather than money solves them.

Several unknowns should shape the build without blocking it. The official judging rubric is unpublished; container and sandbox latencies, the leak guard's false-rejection rate and every threshold in the learner model are unmeasured defaults; and the LeetCode integration rests on an undocumented endpoint whose terms forbid scraping. Each has a cheap answer: benchmark the runner at T+02:00, run a small persuasion-and-injection red-team set against the hint pipeline, and put seeded demo data behind every external call.
